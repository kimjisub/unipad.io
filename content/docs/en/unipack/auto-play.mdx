The `autoPlay` file defines the auto-play sequence.
This data is used in auto-play mode and practice mode.
The text encoding follows the same rules as `keySound`.

## Commands

| Command | Shorthand | Syntax | Description |
|---------|-----------|--------|-------------|
| `on` | `o` | `on x y` | Button press (play sound + start LED) |
| `off` | `f` | `off x y` | Button release |
| `touch` | `t` | `touch x y` | Press + immediate release (on + off in one line) |
| `chain` | `c` | `chain N` | Switch to chain N |
| `delay` | `d` | `delay ms` | Wait in milliseconds |

## Behavior

- Events are executed sequentially from top to bottom.
- The `on` event plays the sound at the given coordinate and advances to the next sound in the circular queue.
- A sound's wormhole switches the chain 100 ms of song time after its `on` or `touch`. Until then, presses continue the original chain's sound queues. A press without a valid sound mapping on that chain is silent and does not bring a pending switch forward.
- Whenever the chain changes — by `chain`, a wormhole, a `chain` event in an LED file, or the user — every button of that chain starts its circular queue from the first sound and LED again, even when the new chain is the same as before.

An explicit `chain` command cancels pending auto-play switches and selects its chain. Stopping auto-play cancels pending switches. A `delay` may be split at a switch time without extending the song; switches still pending at normal end of file are applied at the final song time. Auto-play and practice use the same interpretation.

## Example

```
chain 1
on 1 1
on 1 2
delay 100
off 1 1
off 1 2
delay 200
touch 2 1
touch 2 2
delay 100
chain 2
on 1 1
delay 500
off 1 1
```

<Callout title="Notes">
- Consecutive events without `delay` are executed simultaneously.
- Lines with out-of-range coordinates are skipped.
- In practice mode, chain switches automatically pause so the user can practice at their own pace.
</Callout>

## Chain and input ownership

The shared format applies parsed chain switches at their calculated song time; an auto-play press must not also schedule the manual sound's 100 ms switch. Stopping auto-play cancels its pending changes. Before each scripted press or release, auto-play restores the chain calculated from its file; a player's chain selection or an LED `chain` event lasts only until that next event.
Manual and auto-play releases end only what their own press currently owns. App updates are aligning these overlapping-input and wormhole rules.

## Presses right after a wormhole

In the revised shared format, auto-play and practice switch chains 100 ms of song time after the wormhole press. Manual play switches 100 ms after the press. A press within those 100 ms still plays from the original chain; if its pad has no sound on that chain, it is silent.

To make such a press play from the new chain, add `chain N` after the wormhole press, directly before the first press that should play from chain N. The `chain` line selects chain N at that point and cancels the pending switch. It adds no delay, so every press keeps its song time.

Add one `chain` line per switch, not one before every press. Any chain change, even to the chain already selected, restarts that chain's sound queues from the first sound, so a pad pressed twice in that stretch would play its first sound twice.

Example: `t 4 8` has a wormhole to chain 3, and the `t 5 8` 2 ms later should play chain 3's sound. Add `chain 3` once, before the first `t 5 8`:

```
t 4 8
d 2
chain 3
t 5 8
d 1
t 5 1
d 2
t 4 1
d 66
t 5 8
```

## Completion and editing

The final delay is part of auto-play, including a pause during that wait. Completion releases only auto-play-owned inputs. See [Playback behavior](playback) for interruptions, explicit resume and auto-map with backup and restore.
